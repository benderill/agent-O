Surrogate and Acquisition

An automated lab wants to match a target shade of blue by adding water to 1 ml of dye. Every mix takes time, so it cannot try every amount. Two ideas decide which amount it tries next.

Pipette

Chooses how much water to add.

→
Beaker

1 ml of blue dye plus that water.

→
Camera

Reads the mixed color and reports ΔE against this target shade. Lower is closer.

Surrogate model

After a few mixes you know ΔE at only a handful of water amounts. A surrogate model fills in the gaps. For any amount of water it predicts the ΔE, and a good one also says how sure it is. Asking the surrogate costs nothing, unlike a real mix.

Acquisition function

The surrogate says what it believes. The acquisition function turns that belief into a score for every possible water amount, and the highest score is the next mix. The score balances two pulls: try where the predicted ΔE is low, and try where the model is unsure.

How they work together

  1. Fit the surrogateTrain it on every mix so far.
  2. Score every amountRun the acquisition function over all water amounts. Both steps cost almost nothing.
  3. Mix at the top scoreThe pipette adds that water and the camera reads the color.
  4. Add the reading and repeatStop when the camera reports a match or the budget runs out.
